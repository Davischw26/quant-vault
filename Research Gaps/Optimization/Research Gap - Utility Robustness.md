# Research Gap - Utility Robustness

## Open questions

The article asks whether wider classes of continuous strictly increasing utilities are distinguished by their optimal strategies and how “closeness” of utility classes should translate into closeness of choices and consequences. [[Concave Utilities are Distinguished by their Optimal Strategies#4. QUESTIONS FOR FURTHER INVESTIGATION|Thorp & Whitley (1972), open questions]]

It shows that nearby utilities can produce very different optimal allocations while still producing nearly optimal consequences for one another. [[Concave Utilities are Distinguished by their Optimal Strategies#4. QUESTIONS FOR FURTHER INVESTIGATION|Thorp & Whitley (1972), open questions]]

## Research design

Define a normalized utility neighborhood, optimize every member over the same opportunity set, compare allocation dispersion and utility regret, and distinguish unstable actions from stable consequences.
